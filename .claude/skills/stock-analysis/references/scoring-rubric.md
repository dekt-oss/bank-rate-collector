# Scoring Rubric

## Core rules

- Score each component from 0 to 10 using current evidence.
- Use 5 as genuinely balanced or average, not as a placeholder for missing data.
- Use `N/A` when evidence is insufficient — for ANY component, including critical ones. Never enter a placeholder score without evidence.
- If a critical component (`valuation`, `earnings_outlook`, `business_quality`, `downside_resilience`) is `N/A`: report the partial fundamental score, do NOT produce an overall purchase-attractiveness score, set analysis status to `insufficient_evidence`, and list the missing components with the reason each could not be scored.
- Non-critical `N/A` (e.g., `catalysts`, `capital_allocation`, timing inputs): renormalize available weights, lower coverage/confidence, and name the missing item — this alone does not make the analysis provisional.
- Keep facts and scores separate: first state evidence, then the score implication.
- Do not award the same fact in multiple categories unless it has distinct economic effects.
- Scores summarize the next 12 months; discuss 3-year structural potential separately.

## Fundamental attractiveness

### 1. Valuation and scenario payoff — 25%

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Expensive under reasonable methods; bear/base downside dominates; valuation depends on implausible growth or margins. |
| 3–4 | Premium is difficult to justify; limited upside and meaningful downside; historical or peer comparison is unfavorable after quality adjustment. |
| 5–6 | Roughly fair value; balanced scenario payoff; discount or premium is explainable by fundamentals. |
| 7–8 | Attractive risk/reward under multiple defensible methods; base-case upside is meaningful and downside is bounded. |
| 9–10 | Rare dislocation with strong balance-sheet support, multiple independent valuation supports, and highly asymmetric payoff. |

### 2. Earnings outlook and revisions — 20%

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Material deterioration, repeated misses, severe negative revisions, or fragile guidance. |
| 3–4 | Weak growth, negative revision breadth, margin pressure, or poor estimate visibility. |
| 5–6 | Stable or mixed outlook; estimates broadly unchanged; moderate visibility. |
| 7–8 | Above-peer growth, improving margins or cash flow, broad upward revisions, and credible guidance. |
| 9–10 | Multi-period acceleration with unusually high visibility and positive revision breadth not yet fully reflected in price. |

### 3. Business quality and financial resilience — 20%

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Going-concern, refinancing, accounting, dilution, or severe concentration risk; persistently value-destructive returns. |
| 3–4 | Weak cash conversion, low pricing power, high leverage, cyclically fragile economics, or poor governance. |
| 5–6 | Average durability and balance sheet; returns near cost of capital; manageable but relevant weaknesses. |
| 7–8 | Durable demand, pricing power, strong cash conversion, healthy balance sheet, and returns above cost of capital. |
| 9–10 | Exceptional moat, recurring cash economics, resilient balance sheet, and long record of high incremental returns. |

### 4. Catalysts and monetization — 15%

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Story relies on rumor, undefined TAM, or technology with no credible commercialization path. |
| 3–4 | Early option value with weak customer, capacity, economics, or timing evidence; market already prices substantial success. |
| 5–6 | Plausible catalysts with mixed evidence or long timing; partial price reflection. |
| 7–8 | Specific milestones, customers, capacity, economics, and timing; credible path to earnings or cash flow. |
| 9–10 | Multiple near- to medium-term catalysts with contractual or operating proof and limited price reflection. |

### 5. Downside resilience and thesis risk — 15%

Score high when downside is contained and falsification risk is measurable.

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Large permanent-loss risk, binary outcome, weak liquidity, or thesis cannot be monitored. |
| 3–4 | Material downside and limited protection; risk triggers are hard to observe or control. |
| 5–6 | Balanced risk; identifiable downside and manageable balance sheet; thesis has clear watchpoints. |
| 7–8 | Strong downside protection, flexible balance sheet, diversified drivers, and clear invalidation thresholds. |
| 9–10 | Exceptional asset or cash protection with modest plausible downside and highly observable risk controls. |

### 6. Capital allocation and shareholder return — 5%

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Persistent dilution, value-destructive acquisitions, uncovered payout, or poor governance. |
| 3–4 | Inconsistent allocation, gross buybacks offset by compensation, or low-return reinvestment. |
| 5–6 | Adequate reinvestment and payout; no major value creation or destruction. |
| 7–8 | Disciplined reinvestment, net share reduction, sustainable payout, or debt reduction. |
| 9–10 | Exceptional record of high-return reinvestment and countercyclical capital return. |

## Entry timing

### 1. Technical setup — 40%

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Broken trend, repeated failed support, high gap risk, and no stabilization evidence. |
| 3–4 | Weak relative strength, unfavorable moving-average structure, or downside momentum. |
| 5–6 | Mixed or range-bound setup; balanced support and resistance. |
| 7–8 | Constructive trend, favorable risk/reward near support or confirmed breakout with manageable extension. |
| 9–10 | Rarely attractive setup with strong confirmation, low invalidation distance, and supportive breadth. |

Do not reward a severely overextended price solely because momentum is strong.

### 2. Flows and positioning — 30%

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Persistent informed selling, crowded long positioning, or worsening short/ownership signals. |
| 3–4 | Weak or deteriorating flows with no offsetting evidence. |
| 5–6 | Mixed, neutral, or lagged positioning data. |
| 7–8 | Sustained multi-window accumulation or favorable positioning reset. |
| 9–10 | Strong confirmed accumulation with low crowding and supportive cross-market evidence. |

### 3. Event setup — 20%

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Imminent binary downside, unrealistic expectations, or adverse financing/regulatory event. |
| 3–4 | Negative event skew or high expectations with limited room for upside surprise. |
| 5–6 | Balanced event calendar and expectation bar. |
| 7–8 | Favorable catalysts with a beatable expectation bar and clear timing. |
| 9–10 | Multiple high-quality near-term catalysts with strongly asymmetric payoff. |

### 4. Short-term sentiment — 10%

Use analyst tone, options, short interest, retail attention, and news sentiment only as secondary evidence.

| Score zone | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Euphoric/crowded sentiment or severe unresolved controversy. |
| 3–4 | Unfavorable sentiment and poor reaction function. |
| 5–6 | Balanced or contradictory sentiment. |
| 7–8 | Improving sentiment from a reset base without extreme crowding. |
| 9–10 | Rare capitulation-to-recovery setup confirmed by fundamentals and flows. |

## Overall labels

- **8.5–10.0**: very high attractiveness
- **7.0–8.4**: positive / staged entry
- **5.5–6.9**: neutral
- **4.0–5.4**: low attractiveness
- **below 4.0**: very low attractiveness

Do not translate the label into a personalized position size unless the user explicitly requests portfolio advice.

## Evidence confidence

Score four confidence inputs from 0 to 10 (coverage is computed automatically; the other three use these anchors):

- source quality: 35%;
- weighted data coverage: 30%;
- freshness: 20%;
- cross-source consistency: 15%.

**Source quality anchors**

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Nearly all load-bearing figures from filings, IR, exchange, or official government sources |
| 7-8 | Load-bearing figures from primary sources; some consensus or major-press supplements |
| 5-6 | Some load-bearing figures rely on aggregator sites or a single secondary source |
| 3-4 | Main conclusions rest on unverified secondary sources |
| 0-2 | Unclear provenance, rumor, or unresolved conflicting sources |

**Freshness anchors**

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Price, financials, consensus, and news all current |
| 7-8 | Core data current; some supporting data stale |
| 5-6 | Financials or consensus lag by a quarter or more |
| 3-4 | Core data several quarters old |
| 0-2 | Too stale to support a current judgment |

**Consistency anchors**

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Figures and direction agree across major sources |
| 7-8 | Minor differences with no effect on conclusions |
| 5-6 | Some load-bearing figures conflict but the range is explainable |
| 3-4 | Unresolved conflicts in load-bearing data |
| 0-2 | No comparable basis across sources |

Convert the result — grade boundaries are judged on the UNROUNDED weighted score (a raw 8.49 is B even though it displays as 8.5):

- A: 8.5–10.0
- B: 7.0–8.4
- C: 5.5–6.9
- D: below 5.5

Regardless of source quality, freshness, and consistency, overall data coverage caps the grade (coverage informs the score AND caps the grade by design — a comprehensive grade requires comprehensive scope):

| Weighted coverage | Maximum grade |
|---|---|
| 85%+ | A |
| 70-84% | B |
| 50-69% | C |
| below 50% | D |

Apply these caps:

- no verified reference price: do not issue a current overall score;
- missing both valuation and forward-outlook evidence: confidence no higher than D;
- missing either valuation or forward-outlook evidence: confidence no higher than C;
- thesis depends materially on an unverified rumor: catalyst score no higher than 4 and confidence no higher than C;
- unresolved source conflict in a load-bearing figure: confidence no higher than C unless the range itself is analyzed.

## Timing coverage gates

Renormalizing a nearly-empty timing dimension lets one minor signal impersonate the whole entry-timing judgment. Apply:

| Timing data coverage | Treatment |
|---|---|
| below 50% | Show the partial timing score as reference only; do NOT produce an overall score (`overall_basis: fundamental_only`, status `partial`) |
| 50-69% | Overall may be produced; status is at best `provisional` |
| 70%+ | `complete` possible when other conditions are met |

## Analysis status and provisional criteria

`provisional` is true when: overall is not producible, OR a critical component is missing, OR weighted coverage < 0.85, OR timing coverage < 0.70, OR confidence grade is C/D. A single missing non-critical item (e.g., capital allocation only, coverage >= 0.85, grade A/B) does NOT make the analysis provisional.

| analysis_status | Criteria |
|---|---|
| complete | All critical components scored, coverage >= 0.85, grade A/B |
| provisional | Coverage 0.70-0.85 or grade C, with critical components scored |
| partial | Fundamental scored but timing entirely unavailable (`fundamental_only`) |
| insufficient_evidence | One or more critical components N/A - no overall score |
| restricted | Grade D, or reference price / reporting baseline could not be verified |

When provisional, `restricted`, or `insufficient_evidence`, never present a "very high attractiveness" label; cap at "positive / staged entry" and state why.

## Consistency check

Before publishing, confirm:

- high fundamental score is supported by valuation, earnings, quality, or exceptional asymmetric scenarios—not only a popular narrative;
- high timing score is not based on one trading session;
- a strong long-term thesis can coexist with weak entry timing;
- a weak company can receive a temporary timing score, but the overall 70/30 weighting preserves fundamental primacy;
- the final label matches the base-case payoff and stated risks.
